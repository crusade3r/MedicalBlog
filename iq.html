What IQ is and its relevance

Introduction:

If I asked you what you thought IQ was and what it stood for, there is a high chance that you would not be able to return a correct answer. However, many have a vague idea of how it may affect our lives, or what it means for a person's future. Many are fully aware of its importance and yet it is highly overshadowed in school as it is not something that is required to know at all. I for one was intrigued by IQ and why it is so prominent in our lives, even though it isn't talked about often at academic stages, which is why I chose to write this blog.

IQ stands for intelligence quotient1, which essentially means one's ability to reason, solve problems and learn information relative to the general population. It is calculated using a standardized test which measures a person's cognitive ‘quality’/intelligence. The average score is 100 while the majority of people score between 85 - 115. It is called a quotient as older tests divided a person's ‘mental age’ by their current chronological age, and then multiplied it by 100 for a final score. When modern science found a better way to calculate IQ, the name never changed2.

On the topic of modern IQ tests, they work differently from academic course tests where subject knowledge is tested3. This means someone could be doing terribly in school while having an extraordinarily high IQ and the inverse. The Wechsler Adult Intelligence Scale (WAIS) divides intelligence into 4 main factors; Verbal comprehension, Perceptual reasoning, Working Memory and Processing speed. As you can see, these factors are very important in many different aspects of life - e.g. academics, work, quality of life etc -, which is why IQ is used to represent how smart a person is as opposed to grades for example.

A study was conducted where twins got their IQ tested at the same age and time, and both of their IQs were highly similar6. However, contrary to general assumption, IQ actually changes the older you are5. Many assume IQ is defined at birth and your genetics are what leads you to a high or low IQ, yet that is not entirely the case. Genetics play a large part toward IQ (~50% - ~80%), however, there is no 1 single gene for IQ. IQ is influenced by thousands of genes and yet even that does not result in one's whole IQ. Nurture plays a large role in the genetic potential of one's IQ expressed in their lifetime (IQ is polygenic4). A child’s nutrition, the environment they are in, access to education and more can all influence IQ to a certain extent. Additionally, IQ is considered in age groups. Expression of IQ from genes becomes more and more relied upon by your body as you grow older, and so your IQ changes across your lifetime.

IQ is not defined at birth or a measure of how naturally smart someone is, and it does not define your future at all. Some consider it an unfair advantage to a person’s future/career and others consider it a number and nothing else. While IQ isn't a measure of how academically smart someone is or how prepared one is for their future, it is a measure of intelligence and only that.
