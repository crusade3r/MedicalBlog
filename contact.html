What is the contact greeting with the least transmission of pathogens, and how dangerous are contact greetings?

Introduction:

Many people get sick even though they maintain their health relatively well, keeping good hygeine and a healthy diet as common practice throughout their life. The secret to their supposed misfortune may actually lie in the way they greet people. In this short blog post, we will be going over how the three different contact greetings (Fist bump, Hand shake and a high five) transmit pathogens, and which one transmits the least pathogens. Hopefully, by the end of this post, you will be able to more effectively avoid illness, or atleast be able to derrive one of the many reasons on why you may getting sick out of the blue.

Before diving into these contact greetings, we should first take a look at how to identify a good and bad contact greeting. Pathogens (specifically bcteria and viruses), transmit through many different mediums, yet in this blog the only relevant method will be direct physical bridging between 2 parties. Assuming you, the reader/Person A, are a hygenic person, then for the rest of this blog the person transmitting pathogens will be the person you are greeting (Person B). To begin transmission, person B's hand must have some sort of contact with the pathogen first. With bacteria, this is almost guaranteed as bacteria on your hands can replicate rapidly and stay there for a very long time (asexual reproduction allows for prolonged living in a single habitat as long as the habitat has liveable conditions such as optimal living temperature). They also have 'biofilms' which protect them from threats like the immune system and antibiotics. With viruses, it becomes less likely. Person B must have been compromised and recently touched an exposed area where viral particles may be found (mucus), like the mouth and nose. They then must greet Person A with this mucus/heap of viral particles still on their hands. Viruses have no other method of being transmitted via contact due to their acellular existence (they cannot move on their own as they are not a cell, so non-lviing). Now to get sick from transmission, Person A must touch an exposed area themselves (eye/mouth/nose) with the same hand they greeted Person B with. The period of time in which transmission is likely from bacteria is significantly longer than viruses as bacteria have the ability to live on your hand for much longer (biofilms and abiotic reproduction - binary fission). This is why washing your hands is important.

The first greeting we will look at is the more promising one; the 'Hi'/High five greeting (as of writing this I am unsure if there is an official name for a high five so i'll likely use both terms). A high five, in short, is a way to greet someone in a celebratory manner by essentially using someone else's hand to clap with you. In doing so, you make a loud noise which is associated with celebration and appraise. The greeting is not a relatively popular one, yet it seems to be on of the least transmitting methods of contact greetings in terms of pathogens (and, in turn, illness).

Of course, high fiving is essentially a 2-person clap, where time of contact between both parties lasts for a very short amount of time, so transmission would assumably be lower. Additionally, the surface area of contact is abount half of a handshake for example, being 100% involved in a handshake as to just 50% of your hand involved (entire hand to entire hand vs palm to palm). Pathogens have both a shorter window to cross between parties, but also a smaller area of pathogens are involved in transmission. These 2 factors greatly lead Hi fives to their well earned spot of the 2nd least transmitting contact greeting (of the three to be discussed).
